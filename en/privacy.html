Privacy policy

Trackbook app and this website · Version of 18 October 2026

Trackbook is a training log. There is no account, and Trackbook runs no server for your notebook. It stays on your phone unless you switch on iCloud sync on an iPhone or iPad (section 4). This policy explains which data leaves the phone, when, why, and who receives it. The German version is the binding one.

2. Your notebook on the phone

Workouts, sets, routines, exercises, equipment, gyms, body weight, your name, unit and weekly goal are stored on your device, in the app's own storage, and also in your iCloud only if you switch on iCloud sync (section 4). We cannot see them. On the device they stay until you clear the notebook in Settings or delete the app. You can export them any time as CSV or as a backup file (Settings › Your data). If your phone's own backup (iCloud or Google) is switched on, the system includes the notebook in it, under Apple's or Google's terms. Data from Apple Health or Health Connect is kept out of it (section 3).

3. Apple Health and Health Connect (optional, off by default)

Only if you switch it on in Settings does Trackbook exchange data with Apple Health (iPhone) or Health Connect (Android) on your phone. Each part is switched on separately:

  • Workouts: Trackbook writes every finished workout (type, start, end, name) there. To avoid adding a workout your watch already recorded, the app reads other apps' workouts at the same time. Heart rate and active calories that a watch or chest strap measured during the workout are shown with it; these values are not stored. The iPhone app writes no calories.
  • Apple Watch: if you use Trackbook on an Apple Watch, it asks for access to Apple Health on the watch the first time a workout runs there. During the workout the watch measures your heart rate and shows it. It saves the workout to Apple Health, with the heart rate and active calories it measured, only when Workouts is switched on, and otherwise discards it; the iPhone then does not add the workout a second time. The watch exchanges the running workout (sets, weights, reps, rest) only with your iPhone, directly over Apple's connection between the two; heart rate and calories go neither to the iPhone nor anywhere else.
  • Body weight: Trackbook writes the weight you enter from then on, and reads weight from other apps (such as a scale) to show it in your profile.

This is health data (Art. 9 GDPR). It is processed only on your phone (and your Apple Watch, if you use Trackbook there): data from Apple Health or Health Connect never goes into iCloud sync or into Trackbook's backup files, and is neither shared nor used for advertising. Weights read from there, the record of what Trackbook wrote there and the Health switches are kept in a separate part of the app's storage that your phone's own backups (iCloud, Google or a computer) leave out, as does the transfer to a new Android phone, so on a phone set up from such a backup, Health is off until you switch it on there. Backups made before October 2026 can still contain them. Legal basis: your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR), given with the switch and the system's access dialog. You can withdraw it at any time: switch it off (Trackbook can then remove what it wrote), or revoke access in Apple Health or Health Connect. Weights read from there are deleted from the app when you switch it off. What Trackbook wrote to Apple Health or Health Connect is managed there, under Apple's or Google's terms.

4. iCloud sync (optional, iPhone and iPad only, off by default)

What: when you switch on iCloud sync in Settings and give your consent, Trackbook stores your notebook in your private iCloud database (CloudKit) with Apple, under your Apple ID, so you have it on your iPhones and iPads signed in with the same Apple ID. This covers workouts, sets and notes, routines, custom exercises, equipment, gyms (with their location if you saved one), the body weight and body measurements you enter, and your profile (such as name, unit and weekly goal). It counts against your iCloud storage. Data from Apple Health is never synced to iCloud; it stays in separate storage on the device only (section 3). Android and the web have no sync.

Who: iCloud is provided by Apple, for users in the EU by Apple Distribution International Ltd., Cork, Ireland. Apple's iCloud terms and Apple's privacy policy at apple.com/legal/privacy apply. We have no access to your iCloud data and run no server of our own for it.

Encryption: the data is encrypted in transit, and Trackbook stores the content of every record as encrypted CloudKit fields. Each record's technical name (its type and a random ID) and the times it was created and changed are not encrypted, as for every CloudKit record, so Apple can see them. The content is end-to-end encrypted only if you have turned on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud in your iCloud settings. Otherwise Apple manages the keys, as it does for most iCloud data.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Training data, body weight and body measurements can say something about your health; as far as they are health data, Trackbook syncs them only with your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). The app asks for it when you switch on iCloud sync. Your consent is recorded only on your phone.

Withdrawing consent and deleting: switch off iCloud sync in Trackbook's Settings, and the app stops syncing. It then offers to delete the iCloud copy for all your devices. You can also delete it in the iOS Settings app: [your name] › iCloud › Manage Storage, then choose Trackbook and delete its data (the exact menu names vary between iOS versions). See also Delete your data. Withdrawal does not affect anything done before it.

How long: the iCloud copy stays until you delete it. Switching sync off on its own does not delete it.

5. App updates

When it starts, the app asks Expo's update service whether a newer version of its code is available, and downloads it if so. The request contains your IP address, the platform, the app version and update channel, and a random ID for this installation that Expo uses to roll out updates. It contains nothing from your notebook.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in delivering bug fixes and security updates (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); this is strictly necessary for the service you use (§ 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG).

Recipient: 650 Industries, Inc. (Expo), Palo Alto, California, USA, as our processor, based on the Standard Contractual Clauses. Expo keeps request logs for a limited time.

6. Gym map

When you open the gym map or search for gyms, the app loads map tiles and gym locations from OpenStreetMap-based services. They receive your IP address and the map area you are looking at, which can reveal roughly where you are. Your exact GPS position and your notebook are not sent to these services. The map is only used when you open it; location permission is asked for only then, and you can use the map without it. While the map is open on Wi-Fi, the app also downloads the map around your starred gyms so it works offline; the tile service then sees those areas.

  • Map tiles: OpenFreeMap (openfreemap.org)
  • Gym data, via the Overpass API: overpass-api.de (FOSSGIS e.V., Germany), overpass.private.coffee and overpass.kumi.systems (tried only if the first one doesn't answer)

Legal basis: providing the feature you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). These services act as independent controllers; their own privacy policies apply. Gyms found are cached on your phone.

Street addresses: gyms from the map use their OpenStreetMap address. When you save a gym you placed yourself, or the app placed one for you at the start of a workout, its street address is looked up once with your phone's address service, which Apple (iPhone) or Google (Android) runs under its own responsibility; it receives the saved point (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

At the start of a workout: if you already allowed location, the app checks on your phone whether you are at one of your gyms, and saves your position for a gym that has none yet. Nothing else is sent for this than the address lookup above.

7. App stores and notifications

Downloading the app goes through Apple's App Store or Google Play, which process data under their own responsibility. Rest-timer alerts and the Live Activity are created on your phone; no push service is used.

11. Your rights

You have the right to access your data (Art. 15 GDPR), to correct it (Art. 16), to have it deleted (Art. 17), to restrict processing (Art. 18), to data portability (Art. 20) and to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21). You can withdraw any consent at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)). Most of this you can do yourself in the app: edit or delete entries, export your notebook, switch off iCloud sync and delete the iCloud copy. Otherwise write to marvin.klaproth@gmail.com.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), for example the one in the German state where you live.

12. Security

All connections are encrypted (HTTPS/TLS). Your private iCloud database can only be reached with your Apple ID; Trackbook has no access to it. For encryption in iCloud, see section 4.

13. Changes

We update this policy when the app changes. If a change affects what you consented to, the app asks you again.
